How a session ages the ground

A long recording should not look like a short one. The ground warms as the session runs — seed hues pull toward 40° and saturation lifts. The only open question is the shape of that curve over time. Three things fall out. The curves are identical at minute zero and identical again from minute sixty — the whole decision lives between three and forty-five minutes, which is why this page now plays to 45 and rests at 8. Linear and stepped are not a real choice (ΔE 0.1–6.3, mostly under threshold); the choice is asymptotic or not. And dark mode halves every delta — 10.0 average against 20.8 in light — because the band is compressed down there and warming has less room to move.

Still asymptotic. It is the only curve that gives a five-minute memo a look of its own (43% warm, against 11% and 0%), and it has no cliff — nothing changes at a moment that reads as an event, which matters because ember is the app’s one signal colour and a warming that announces itself competes with it.

Two rules the warming keeps whatever curve wins

  • It never reaches ember. Warm anchor is 40°, ember is 22° light / 30° dark, and saturation caps well under the accent’s. The ground getting warm may never be mistaken for the microphone being open.
  • It is monotonic and it never resets mid-session. Warmth is a function of elapsed time alone — not of speech, not of markers, not of scroll. Anything that could go backwards reads as a bug in a surface whose whole job is to be ambient.
enum Warming {
  // t in seconds; returns 0...1
  static func asymptotic(_ t: TimeInterval) -> Double {
    1 - exp(-t / 540)          // 9-minute time constant
  }
}
